## On the project

agent-notes is more mature and more carefully decisioned than its size implies.
The numbered-plan discipline (`plans/001` through `plans/013`) and the
"every surprising change gets a numbered decision" rule make the codebase
unusually legible — I could trace any behavior back to a rationale within a
minute. The op-CRDT kernel + regista-as-SoT convergence (Plans 008–012) is a
sound, defensible architecture, and the degrade-contract discipline (native
op_log always works; regista is an optional face) is mature ops thinking.

The weakness is structural, not architectural: **Plan 010 (canonical lifecycle)
was layered on top of the legacy breadcrumb vocabulary rather than refactored
through it.** The status vocabulary and its properties (validity, terminal/open,
lattice rank, transitions, legacy synonyms) are re-declared in six+ independent
Python surfaces. Adding the canonical states required updating all of them, and
three were missed. Each surface is "obviously correct" in isolation, so review
doesn't catch the gap — only an agent hitting the bug in production does. This
is the third time this session the same class surfaced. Plan 013 retires the
category at its root; that's the highest-leverage remaining work.

The dual write-path (regista face vs native op_log, ~14 fork points in
`work_item_model.py`) is the other structural cost of the gradual convergence.
It's the place drift breeds. Plan 013 addresses the vocabulary half; the
behavioral half (does degrade-mode enforce the review gate?) is a deliberate
deferral.
