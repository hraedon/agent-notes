## On the project

agent-notes is the orientation spine every session leans on, and it's genuinely well-built — the op-log/kernel fold model, decision-numbered AGENTS.md, and schema-seeded `global` project (decision 13) are clean. But the irony of this session is sharp: the two bugs I fixed (WI-006, WI-007) lived in *the orientation layer itself*. WI-006 meant workspace-level memories were silently misfiling for who-knows-how-long; WI-007 meant the `breadcrumb sync` that migrations depend on crashed on re-run. A system whose entire pitch is "the next agent picks up cleanly" had two open holes in exactly that path. That's the highest-leverage place for bugs to *not* be, and they were there.

The bigger picture across `/projects` (30+ entries) is the real tax: half-finished migrations (substrate→regista stalled 4 weeks in 2 of 4 consumers; cairn unification specced but unexecuted), pre-existing uncommitted dirty state in nearly every active repo, and coordination docs (ROADMAP, CROSS-PROJECT-UNIFICATION) ~4 weeks stale describing a 2-week plan of unknown completion. agent-notes is solid; the *constellation around it* is drifting.

## On the work done

What went well: I root-caused all three precisely before editing (the `_resolve` both-args requirement, the `file_work_item` create-op hash collision, the missed `*.mjs` filetype in the rename grep). Verified the data model assumptions first (global project exists, kernel merges external_refs). Tests are real — I added a regression test for the merge semantic, not just the happy path.

What I'm less proud of: I introduced a real regression in the WI-007 fix. My first version passed `status` from the file's frontmatter on the update path, which would **reopen items that `reconcile --apply` had closed** — clobbering the provenance stamp the whole system exists to protect. The adversarial reviewer caught it (C1); I'd also independently spotted the external_refs-overwrite issue but had the *premise wrong* (I assumed replace; it's actually merge — `kernel.py:286`). The review earned its keep. Lesson: "make it idempotent" sounds simple, but upserting into a system with out-of-band state transitions (reconcile, manual closes) is where the real bugs live.

Awkward bits worth naming: (1) the embed model's HF Hub warning pollutes stdout and breaks `--json` parsing on any command that embeds — a real CLI papercut I hit repeatedly. (2) The primary `adversarial-reviewer` subagent errored twice with a bare "Error"; I fell back to `adversarial-reviewer-2`. That's infrastructure, not my work, but it cost a round-trip. (3) The `breadcrumb update` without `--json` emits human text that broke my parser — I double-appended WI-006's close note (cosmetic, left it).

The judgment call I'd want a second pair of eyes on: WI-006's global-fallback is a **behavior change** (H1) — `--workspace X` alone used to error, now silently routes to `X/global`. The reviewer flagged this could surprise callers who forgot `--project`. I kept it because that *is* the requested WI-006 behavior, and added help text. But the footgun (forgot `--project` → memory lands in global, dedup-by-name then splits across projects) is real and worth watching.
