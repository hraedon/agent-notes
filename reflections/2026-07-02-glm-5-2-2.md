## On the project

The suite-cohesion arc is the most substantial thing happening across this
family right now: one blueprint, six per-component cohesion plans (regista
025-028, dossier 013-016, agent-notes 017, cairn 008, acb 005, wake 004), and
the new `agent-suite` orchestration layer (plan 001) — all specified before
implementation, which is the right way around for a regulated-deployment
story. The contracts-first discipline (install-harness-contract.md defined
*before* any component builds theirs) is exactly what stops the four
harness-wiring commands from becoming four dialects.

`agent-suite` itself is a refreshing codebase to land in: stdlib-only core,
`mypy --strict` from day one, `assert_never` on every closed-set dispatch, and
a mechanical architecture test that the core imports no backend SDK and no
component code. The "thin orchestration, never reimplement" rule is the thing
that will keep it from accreting a second divergent implementation — the
temptation to "just do it here" is real and the AGENTS.md names it honestly.

## On the work done

The doctor stub is the part I'm most confident in. The injectable
`installed`/`runner` seam means the aggregation logic is fully testable
against stubbed component doctors with no real binaries on PATH — the
architecture test even guards that the core never imports a component. The
honest-health gating (installed-but-broken → failure; spine absent or
all-absent → not ok; absent Tier-2 → named, not a failure) matches the
bootstrap-contract §3 wording, and the parametrized exhaustiveness test over
the status enum proves the `assert_never` is real, not decorative.

The plan-017 gap analysis is the higher-leverage artifact of the two. It
establishes that `install-harness` is a *superset* of the existing
`install-skills` (skills are done and reusable; the env/plugin/hook wiring is
the new layer), which means WI-2.1 is mostly additive, not a rewrite. The
sequencing call — WI-1.1 (canonical `REGISTA_*`) before WI-2.1 — is
load-bearing: you can't wire `REGISTA_DSN` into a harness env block before the
config-resolution precedence exists.

What I'd want a second pair of eyes on: the `suite_ok` gating policy is a
judgment call where the contract is silent. I made absent Tier-2 non-failing
(explicit in the contract) but also allowed absent *faces* to not fail the
suite (a suite with only a spine reports `suite_ok:true` with faces showing
`absent`). That's defensible — doctor reports what's deployed and healthy —
but a stricter reader might want absent core faces to fail. It's a stub, so
easily revisited, but worth flagging.
